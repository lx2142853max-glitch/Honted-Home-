THE CURSED MANSION: THREE SEALS HAUNTED ESTATE – ENVIRONMENT
REVERSE-ENGINEERING & LEVEL DESIGN DOCUMENT Version 1.0 | Concept based
on the supplied mansion reference image

====================================================================== 1.
DESIGN INTENT
======================================================================

This document treats the supplied picture as a visual reference, not as
a real architectural survey. The visible building is a large
Victorian/Gothic mansion on a raised rocky plot, surrounded by a dead
garden, iron fencing, a long entrance path, gravestones, bare trees and
heavy night fog. The design below expands that image into a playable,
interconnected horror-adventure level.

Core design goals: - The mansion must feel much larger inside than it
appears from the front. - The player should be able to explore, get
lost, discover shortcuts and gradually understand the building. - The
house is not just a boss arena. It is the central world hub connecting
three villain territories. - Every locked door, symbol, strange sound
and unusual object should have a gameplay purpose. - At least three
complete floors, a basement network, an attic and outdoor grounds are
required. - All supernatural systems are fictional game mechanics.

Suggested game camera: first-person or over-the-shoulder third-person.
Suggested structure: one continuous estate map divided by locked gates,
keys, puzzles and boss progression. Suggested mood: cold moonlight, deep
blue-grey shadows, occasional green/orange window light, wind, rain,
distant bells and floor creaks.

======================================================================
2. WHAT CAN BE READ FROM THE REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Visible visual clues: 1. A tall central tower rises above the rest of
the mansion. 2. The building has multiple steep roofs, pointed gables,
dormer windows and narrow turrets. 3. The front entrance sits above
ground level and is reached by a long stone path and steps. 4. A covered
porch or veranda runs along part of the front. 5. Several windows glow
green, while a few glow warm orange. 6. The mansion sits on an uneven
rocky mound rather than a flat lawn. 7. An iron fence and gate define
the front boundary. 8. The foreground contains a cracked path, dead
shrubs, bare trees and gravestones. 9. Fog and a dark forest conceal the
estate boundary. 10. The moon and storm clouds create a clear night-time
silhouette.

Design interpretation: - Green windows suggest a sealed laboratory,
cursed library or supernatural power room. - Orange windows suggest
ordinary lanterns, an old caretaker room or a misleading safe-looking
location. - The tower is the strongest landmark and should be visible
from the front garden, courtyard and some upper-floor windows. - The
raised rocky foundation makes a lower crypt and service tunnel plausible
as fictional level spaces. - The graveyard should connect to the house
through a side gate and a hidden underground route.

Do not make every window accessible. Some should be decorative
silhouettes; others should reveal rooms the player can later reach from
inside.

======================================================================
3. ESTATE MAP – BIG-PICTURE LAYOUT
======================================================================

The estate is divided into eight connected regions:

A. Front Approach and Gate B. Front Garden and Graveyard C. Main Mansion
– three floors D. West Wing – puppet and doll territory E. East Wing –
spirit and archive territory F. Rear Garden, greenhouse and ruined
chapel G. Underground Basement, crypt and hidden tunnel network H.
Central Tower and sealed upper chamber

Suggested navigation: - The front gate leads to the front path. - The
front path leads to the porch and main entrance. - The main entrance
opens into the grand hall. - The grand hall connects to the west wing,
east wing, main staircase and rear conservatory. - The west wing
contains Morvex-related clues and the doll workshop. - The east wing
contains Vorak-related clues and the spirit archive. - The basement
connects to the kitchen stores, boiler room, crypt and hidden tunnel. -
The rear garden connects to the greenhouse, chapel ruins and a concealed
tunnel entrance. - The third floor connects to the central tower. - The
tower contains the final seal mechanism and clues about Grimthor.

The player should gradually unlock loops. For example, a basement
service door can open back into the kitchen, creating a shortcut instead
of forcing the player to repeat the whole route.

======================================================================
4. OUTDOOR GROUNDS – FULL DETAIL
======================================================================

4.1 OUTER FOREST - A narrow muddy trail leads from the road to the
estate. - Fallen branches, tree roots, rocks and thick fog limit
visibility. - A broken signpost carries a faded mansion emblem. - A
distant bell rings at irregular intervals. - A collapsed stone wall
hints that the estate once covered a much larger area. - Optional
collectible: a torn survey map showing an erased path.

Gameplay: - Teach movement, crouching, looking behind objects and
reading environmental clues. - The forest is a boundary zone, not an
open-world forest. - A locked maintenance gate prevents leaving before
the first story objective.

4.2 FRONT IRON GATE Visual design: - Two tall wrought-iron leaves with
pointed finials. - Central circular crest showing three interlocked
symbols. - Rusted chain and a heavy mechanical lock. - Two stone pillars
topped with weathered lanterns. - A small keeper’s box beside the gate.

Interactions: - Gate is initially locked. - The crest has three empty
recesses for the three Seals. - A small side hatch can be opened later
from the inside. - The gate should visibly react when each Seal is
acquired.

4.3 FRONT PATH AND STEPS - Long uneven cobblestone path, cracked in
places. - Dead weeds grow between stones. - Three pairs of low path
lanterns; only one works at the start. - Stone steps climb toward the
raised porch. - A drain channel runs beside the steps and can later
reveal a basement access clue. - A loose paving stone hides a small key
or note.

4.4 FRONT GARDEN - Dead rose beds, thorn bushes, broken statues and a
dry fountain. - A circular flowerbed has a three-part pattern matching
the gate crest. - A toppled angel statue points toward a hidden ground
marker. - A locked garden shed stores maintenance equipment. - A
birdbath reflects a window that is not visible from the player’s current
position.

4.5 GRAVEYARD - Several uneven gravestones, a family mausoleum and a low
iron fence. - Names and dates tell a small family history. - One grave
has a newer stone among much older graves. - A cracked mausoleum floor
leads to a lower crypt passage after a puzzle. - The graveyard is
connected to the basement tunnel, but the connection is not available
immediately.

Gameplay: - Find grave inscriptions that reveal a combination. - Use the
Phantom Eye or equivalent story item to reveal a hidden symbol. - The
mausoleum is a quiet exploration zone with distant footsteps and moving
shadows.

4.6 SIDE COURTYARD - Narrow passage between mansion and boundary wall. -
Rain barrel, broken cart, stacked firewood and a locked service door. -
A drain grate gives a view into the basement corridor. - A small statue
marks the entrance to the old servant passage.

4.7 REAR GARDEN - Overgrown lawn, dead fountain, tangled hedges and a
collapsed gazebo. - A stone sundial has symbols rather than numbers. - A
garden maze has a simple looping layout, not a frustrating random
maze. - A statue’s shadow points to the greenhouse at a specific in-game
time. - A hidden hatch beneath ivy leads to the tunnel network.

4.8 GREENHOUSE - Broken glass roof, dead vines, empty plant pots and a
rusted irrigation system. - A locked cabinet contains a rare object or
crafting component. - A plant table contains notes about the mansion’s
former owner. - A back door leads to the ruined chapel path.

4.9 RUINED CHAPEL - Small roofless chapel behind the greenhouse. -
Cracked altar, empty candle holders and a bell frame. - Three floor
tiles show the same symbols as the three Boss territories. - A concealed
wall niche contains an old record of the guardians. - This area
foreshadows the final chamber without revealing the whole mystery.

======================================================================
5. MANSION EXTERIOR – ARCHITECTURAL BREAKDOWN
======================================================================

Overall silhouette: - Central tower: tallest element, slightly set back
from the front facade. - Main block: three storeys with irregular
rooflines. - West wing: steep triangular gable and narrow windows. -
East wing: turret and a projecting bay window. - Front porch: covered
entrance with columns and a balcony/veranda above. - Roof: slate
shingles, moss, broken gutters and several chimneys. - Foundation: rough
stone base, visibly raised above the garden.

Suggested fictional scale: - Main building footprint: approximately 42 m
wide x 30 m deep. - Main floor-to-floor height: 4.2 m. - Third floor and
attic spaces: variable 3.2–4 m. - Central tower: approximately 18–22 m
above ground. - Outdoor playable estate: approximately 150 m x 130 m.
These are game-blockout estimates, not measurements taken from the
image.

Exterior details: - Rainwater pipes, cracked stone, ivy, missing roof
tiles and boarded windows. - A few windows have curtains that move even
when there is no wind. - One upper window occasionally shows a
silhouette. - A balcony door is visible but locked from the inside. - A
broken drainpipe gives access to a small ledge, but not a full climbing
system. - Gargoyle-like stone ornaments mark the west and east wings. -
The tower roof has a weather vane shaped like the mansion crest.

======================================================================
6. FLOOR 1 – GROUND FLOOR / PUBLIC ROOMS
======================================================================

Purpose: orientation, first exploration, basic keys and the first links
between wings.

6.1 GRAND ENTRANCE HALL - Double front doors with a heavy internal
bar. - Black-and-white patterned floor. - Large staircase curving to the
second floor. - Three portraits with scratched faces. - A stopped
grandfather clock. - Main chandelier with several missing bulbs. - A
large wall emblem with three empty seal slots. - Doors to the dining
room, west corridor, east corridor and rear conservatory.

Gameplay: - First major navigation hub. - The player learns that the
house has three wings or guardian territories. - The clock hands can be
adjusted later to reveal a hidden compartment.

6.2 RECEPTION / STUDY - Desk, ink bottles, locked drawers and a wall
map. - A torn letter mentions the three guardians. - A drawer contains a
partial mansion plan. - A bookshelf can slide after a book-order
puzzle. - A small safe requires a clue from the graveyard.

6.3 LIBRARY - Two-level bookshelves, rolling ladder and a reading
balcony. - A fireplace with a false back panel. - Books arranged by
symbols, not alphabetically. - One shelf contains a hidden switch. - A
locked archive cabinet stores a guardian journal. - A narrow door
connects to the east wing.

6.4 DINING ROOM - Long table with thirteen chairs. - Dusty plates,
silverware and a broken serving trolley. - Portraits of former
residents. - A dumbwaiter shaft links to the kitchen below. - A place
setting puzzle reveals a small key.

6.5 KITCHEN - Large stove, sink, pantry shelves and hanging utensils. -
Cold fireplace and a locked pantry. - A service stair descends to the
basement. - A loose floorboard hides a note. - The pantry contains food
props and basic crafting supplies.

6.6 CONSERVATORY - Glass ceiling, dead plants and wet stone floor. - A
cracked fountain with a circular symbol. - Rear door opens to the garden
after the latch puzzle. - A green-lit window visible from outside
belongs to a sealed side room nearby.

6.7 WEST CORRIDOR - Narrow corridor with old family photographs. -
Doll-sized footprints appear in dust. - A small locked door leads to the
west wing. - A wall panel hides a shortcut to the grand hall after
Morvex’s first puzzle.

6.8 EAST CORRIDOR - Colder lighting, damp wallpaper and faint
whispering. - Several doors have scratched circular symbols. - Leads
toward the spirit archive and a sealed room linked to Vorak.

======================================================================
7. FLOOR 2 – BEDROOMS / WEST AND EAST WINGS
======================================================================

Purpose: stealth, hiding, more complex puzzles and the first boss
territory access.

7.1 MAIN LANDING - Balcony overlooking the grand hall. - Large mirror
with a covered section. - Doors to bedrooms and both wings. - A locked
cabinet contains a floor plan fragment.

7.2 MASTER BEDROOM - Four-poster bed, wardrobe, dressing table and
fireplace. - A hidden compartment behind a portrait. - A diary explains
why the family abandoned the mansion. - Wardrobe can be used as a hiding
place. - A balcony door looks over the front garden.

7.3 GUEST BEDROOMS - Three rooms with different clues. - One has a
broken music box. - One has a locked trunk. - One has a window facing
the graveyard. - Each room can contain a note, key or puzzle component.

7.4 CHILDREN’S ROOM - Old toys, small beds, drawings and a dollhouse. -
The dollhouse reproduces the mansion layout. - Moving a miniature object
can reveal the location of a real hidden switch. - A small crawlspace
connects to the west wing.

7.5 WEST WING – MORVEX TERRITORY Rooms: - Puppet Gallery: mannequins and
suspended marionettes. - Doll Workshop: worktables, spare parts and
locked cabinets. - Music Room: piano, music cylinders and a
sound-pattern puzzle. - Control Room: a wall diagram of the puppet
network. - Storage Room: broken toys and crafting materials.

Environmental clues: - Strings move when no puppet is visible. - A
music-box melody can be heard in more than one room. - Some dolls change
position when the player looks away. - A service hatch links the
workshop to the basement.

7.6 EAST WING – VORAK TERRITORY Rooms: - Spirit Archive: journals, glass
cases and spectral diagrams. - Seance Room: circular floor pattern and
covered mirrors. - Observatory: telescope pointed toward the moon. -
Cold Chamber: frost-covered walls and a sealed cabinet. - Forgotten
Chapel Room: a small indoor shrine with a missing emblem.

Environmental clues: - Cold air leaks from one wall. - Reflections do
not always match the room. - Faint voices lead toward hidden notes. - A
locked archive door opens after a light-and-symbol puzzle.

7.7 SECOND-FLOOR SERVICE PASSAGE - Narrow passage behind wardrobes and
wall panels. - Connects the west and east wings. - Initially blocked by
a fallen shelf. - Once opened, it creates a useful shortcut between both
territories.

======================================================================
8. FLOOR 3 – ATTIC LEVEL / FORBIDDEN ROOMS
======================================================================

Purpose: advanced lore, dangerous exploration and access to the central
tower.

8.1 ATTIC LANDING - Low beams, dusty trunks and sloped ceilings. - Roof
windows show the storm and tower. - A locked iron door leads to the
central tower stair. - A broken staircase section requires a route
around storage rooms.

8.2 FAMILY ARCHIVE - Old ledgers, photographs, maps and sealed
documents. - The player learns that the guardians once protected the
estate. - A family tree connects the former residents to the pendant. -
A coded page hints that the three Seals are not ordinary keys.

8.3 TAXIDERMY / DISPLAY ROOM - Covered display cases, old hunting
trophies and strange labels. - One case is empty but has a fresh dust
outline. - A clue is hidden behind a display base. - Keep this room
eerie rather than graphic.

8.4 LOCKED OBSERVATION ROOM - Narrow windows overlook the front gate and
graveyard. - A mechanical telescope can be rotated. - Aligning it with
three exterior landmarks reveals a symbol sequence. - The sequence
unlocks a hidden compartment.

8.5 FORBIDDEN STUDY - Desk, sealed books, star chart and a wall of
symbols. - A false bookshelf conceals the entrance to a narrow stair. -
The room contains the first clear reference to the Unknown Fourth
Entity. - The player should not meet the final entity here; only
foreshadow it.

8.6 CENTRAL TOWER STAIR - Spiral stone stair with uneven steps. - Narrow
arrow-slit windows. - A bell rope hangs down but is cut. - Three
landings each show one guardian emblem. - The upper door remains sealed
until all three Boss Seals are obtained.

======================================================================
9. BASEMENT – SERVICE LEVEL AND CRYPT
======================================================================

Purpose: connect all parts of the estate and provide the main hidden
tunnel network.

9.1 BASEMENT STAIR - Access from kitchen and a concealed stair near the
library. - Damp stone walls, pipes and old electrical conduits. - A
flickering lamp and water dripping into a bucket. - A locked maintenance
door.

9.2 BOILER ROOM - Old boiler, pressure gauges, valves and coal
storage. - A safe puzzle uses the gauge positions. - A service tunnel
entrance is visible but blocked. - Restoring the boiler can power
selected mansion mechanisms.

9.3 WINE CELLAR - Rows of dusty bottles, wooden racks and a locked
cage. - Bottle labels form a code. - One rack slides aside to reveal a
narrow passage. - A rare collectible can be placed here.

9.4 STORAGE ROOM - Crates, spare furniture, old tools and covered
objects. - One crate contains the original front-gate crest. - A wall
map reveals the estate’s old underground layout.

9.5 CRYPT - Family tombs, stone shelves and sealed niches. - Three
guardian emblems are carved into the central floor. - A hidden floor
panel opens after a three-symbol puzzle. - This crypt connects to the
graveyard mausoleum.

9.6 HIDDEN TUNNEL NETWORK Tunnel branches: A. Crypt to Graveyard
Mausoleum. B. Boiler Room to Rear Garden drain. C. West Wing workshop to
Storage Room. D. Rear Garden hatch to Ruined Chapel. E. A sealed branch
toward the unknown underground chamber.

Tunnel details: - Low stone ceiling, occasional timber supports and
shallow water. - Old directional plaques show a simple emblem system. -
Some branches are blocked by collapsed masonry. - A few grates let the
player hear rooms above. - The tunnel map is found in pieces, not given
at the start. - Add safe navigation landmarks so the player can learn
the network.

9.7 UNKNOWN UNDERGROUND PLACE – THE NULL CHAMBER - A circular room
beneath the central tower. - No visible door from the tunnel side until
the three Seals are found. - The walls absorb most ambient sound. - Four
empty pedestals surround a dark central plinth. - Three pedestals match
the guardian Seals; the fourth has an unfamiliar symbol. - A broken
mural depicts three guardians facing a shape with no face. - The pendant
reacts here. - This is the gateway to the next chapter, not the final
boss fight itself.

======================================================================
10. CENTRAL TOWER – THE THREE-SEAL SANCTUM
======================================================================

Lower tower: - A narrow landing with a locked iron door. - Three
guardian symbols engraved into the floor. - A small window overlooks the
front gate.

Middle tower: - A bell chamber with a large cracked bell. - Three ropes,
only one connected. - A rotating mechanism aligns the guardian
emblems. - A hidden stair leads to the upper chamber.

Upper tower: - A circular room beneath the roof. - Three Seal holders
around a central stone table. - The player’s pendant fits into a fourth
recess. - The tower weather vane casts a symbol-shaped shadow at
moonrise. - Once the three Seals are placed, the tower reveals the route
to the Null Chamber.

======================================================================
11. HAUNTED SYMBOL SYSTEM
======================================================================

Create a consistent visual language. Symbols should be readable and
reused across doors, floor tiles, notes, statues and puzzle devices.

Symbol A – Soul Spiral: - Three curved lines around a central dot. -
Used by Vorak. - Meaning: spirit movement and memory.

Symbol B – Marionette Knot: - A small diamond with four thread-like
arms. - Used by Morvex. - Meaning: control, connection and repetition.

Symbol C – Broken Crown: - Three upward points with a gap in the
centre. - Used by Grimthor. - Meaning: guardian authority and curse.

Symbol D – House Crest: - A tower inside a circle with three outer
marks. - Used on the front gate and family documents. - Meaning: the
estate itself.

Symbol E – The Unknown Mark: - An incomplete circle with a missing
centre. - Appears only in late-game areas. - Meaning is not explained
until the next chapter.

Symbol placement: - Carved into stone near important doors. - Stamped
into keys and seals. - Scratched into furniture by previous residents. -
Embroidered on old banners. - Hidden in stained-glass window patterns. -
Printed in the margins of journals.

Puzzle rule: - Never use a symbol only as decoration if it looks
important. - Important symbols should repeat at least three times before
the player is expected to understand them. - Provide optional journal
sketches to help players remember them.

======================================================================
12. DOOR TYPES AND LOCKING SYSTEM
======================================================================

1.  Ordinary wooden door: Opens normally; may creak loudly.

2.  Jammed door: Requires a simple environmental interaction or
    alternate route.

3.  Key-locked door: Requires a named key found in the same general
    region.

4.  Symbol-locked door: Requires the correct symbol sequence.

5.  Mechanical door: Requires power restored from the boiler room or a
    gear mechanism.

6.  Hidden door: Revealed by moving a book, mirror, portrait or wall
    panel.

7.  Boss-sealed door: Opens after the corresponding Boss encounter.

8.  One-way shortcut door: Opens from the far side after exploration.

9.  Tunnel hatch: Requires a crank, handle or access key.

10. Final tower door: Requires all three Boss Seals and the pendant.

Door feedback: - Correct key: distinct metal click. - Wrong symbol: low
stone vibration. - Boss seal door: emblem glows briefly. - Hidden door:
dust falls and a mechanical sound plays. - Do not make every locked door
use the same key; each lock type should teach a different rule.

======================================================================
13. INTERCONNECTED BOSS TERRITORIES
======================================================================

VORAK – EAST WING / SPIRIT ARCHIVE - Main area: Seance Room, Spirit
Archive and Cold Chamber. - Supporting area: Observatory and crypt
branch. - Main clue: the Soul Spiral. - Boss arena: a sealed chamber
behind the archive. - Reward: First Seal. - Unlock: a shortcut from the
east wing to the grand hall.

MORVEX – WEST WING / PUPPET WORKSHOP - Main area: Puppet Gallery, Doll
Workshop and Music Room. - Supporting area: Children’s Room and basement
service hatch. - Main clue: the Marionette Knot. - Boss arena: the
hidden theatre beneath the west wing. - Reward: Second Seal. - Unlock:
the service passage between both wings.

GRIMTHOR – TOWER / FORBIDDEN LEVEL - Main area: Forbidden Study, tower
stair and crypt. - Supporting area: ruined chapel and underground
chamber. - Main clue: the Broken Crown. - Boss arena: the upper tower
sanctum. - Reward: Third Seal. - Unlock: access to the Null Chamber.

Each boss area must be reachable through the mansion’s shared corridors
and shortcuts. Do not build three disconnected maps.

======================================================================
14. PLAYER EXPLORATION AND SURVIVAL SYSTEMS
======================================================================

Player actions: - Walk, run, crouch and interact. - Inspect notes,
portraits, symbols and objects. - Hide in wardrobes, under selected beds
and behind large furniture. - Carry a limited number of tools. - Open
and close doors carefully. - Use a journal to record clues and map
discoveries. - Place quest objects in a separate protected inventory
category.

Sound system: - Running on wood creates more noise than walking on
carpet. - Dropping an object can attract a nearby enemy. - Doors have
different sound levels. - Rain and thunder sometimes mask player
movement. - Sound clues should be directional but not unfair.

Hiding system: - Hiding places must be visually understandable. - Some
enemies can inspect a hiding place after a clue or sound. - Do not make
every hiding place safe forever. - Provide a clear escape route from
hiding locations.

Map system: - The player starts with a partial ground-floor plan. - New
areas are drawn after entering them. - Hidden passages appear only after
discovery. - Use consistent icons for locked doors, puzzle devices,
stairs and discovered shortcuts.

Save system: - Save at clear checkpoints or selected safe rooms. - Save
collected quest objects and unlocked shortcuts. - Boss progress must
persist after a checkpoint. - Avoid losing a long exploration session
due to one mistake.

======================================================================
15. LIGHTING, WEATHER AND AUDIO
======================================================================

Lighting: - Moonlight: cool blue-grey exterior fill. - Green window
glow: cursed or supernatural room. - Orange window glow: lantern,
fireplace or misleading safe zone. - Candlelight: small warm pools with
strong moving shadows. - Basement: low visibility but readable
navigation landmarks. - Tower: moonlight through narrow windows and
occasional lightning flashes.

Weather: - Continuous light rain during the opening. - Stronger wind
near the tower. - Occasional thunder, not constantly. - Fog is denser
around the graveyard and rear garden. - Weather changes should be
scripted at story milestones, not random enough to obscure navigation.

Audio: - Wind through broken windows. - Distant bell from the tower. -
Floorboard creaks. - Pipe knocks in the basement. - A music-box phrase
in the west wing. - Faint whispers in the east wing. - Low mechanical
ticking near the tower. - Silence in the Null Chamber.

Audio fairness: - Important sounds should have a recognizable source or
journal explanation. - Do not use loud jumpscares as the only way to
communicate danger. - Use quiet environmental cues to build tension.

======================================================================
16. STORY CLUE PLACEMENT
======================================================================

Use short notes, not long exposition dumps.

Clue 1 – Front Gate: “The house remembers every name written upon its
stones.”

Clue 2 – Study: “Three keepers. Three seals. One door beneath the
tower.”

Clue 3 – Children’s Room: A drawing shows three figures standing around
a fourth empty shape.

Clue 4 – Spirit Archive: The guardians were once protectors, not
monsters.

Clue 5 – Puppet Workshop: The dolls repeat a melody heard before the
mansion was cursed.

Clue 6 – Forbidden Study: The curse came from something the guardians
were trying to contain.

Clue 7 – Crypt: The three Seals are parts of one larger lock.

Clue 8 – Null Chamber: The fourth pedestal is older than the mansion
itself.

Keep the true identity of the Unknown Entity hidden until the next
chapter.

======================================================================
17. GAME PROGRESSION – SUGGESTED ORDER
======================================================================

Opening: 1. Arrive at the outer forest. 2. Find the gate clue. 3. Enter
the front garden. 4. Discover the main entrance. 5. Explore the ground
floor and obtain a partial map.

Early exploration: 6. Unlock the kitchen and basement stairs. 7. Find
the library shortcut. 8. Explore the second-floor bedrooms. 9. Discover
that both wings are sealed by different symbols.

Boss chapter 1: 10. Explore the east wing. 11. Find Vorak’s tools and
clues. 12. Solve the archive puzzles. 13. Complete the Vorak encounter
and obtain the First Seal.

Boss chapter 2: 14. Open the west wing. 15. Explore the doll workshop
and music room. 16. Find Morvex’s tools and clues. 17. Complete the
Morvex encounter and obtain the Second Seal.

Late exploration: 18. Open the service passage and more tunnel branches.
19. Explore the graveyard, greenhouse and ruined chapel. 20. Discover
the Forbidden Study and tower stair.

Boss chapter 3: 21. Find Grimthor’s clues and crafting components. 22.
Solve the tower and crypt puzzles. 23. Complete the Grimthor encounter
and obtain the Third Seal.

Final mystery: 24. Place all three Seals in the tower. 25. Open the Null
Chamber. 26. Discover the fourth pedestal and the Unknown Mark. 27. End
the chapter with a new mystery.

19. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES FOR A GAME DEVELOPER
======================================================================

Build the level in stages:

Stage A – Greybox: - Use simple cubes and modular walls. - Establish
room sizes, corridors, staircases and outdoor distances. - Make sure all
three floors connect correctly. - Test navigation before adding visual
detail.

Stage B – Playable route: - Add doors, keys, locks, stairs, ladders only
where needed, and shortcut loops. - Ensure the player can return to the
grand hall from each wing. - Test the basement and tunnel network for
confusing dead ends.

Stage C – Puzzle logic: - Add symbols, clues, switches and state
tracking. - Each puzzle should have a readable hint. - Make sure quest
progression cannot be permanently blocked.

Stage D – Art pass: - Add Gothic architecture, wood, stone, iron, glass,
moss, ivy and weather damage. - Use repeated modular assets to control
performance. - Keep silhouettes and navigation landmarks clear.

Stage E – Horror pass: - Add lighting, fog, rain, sound cues, enemy
patrol routes and scripted events. - Keep visual effects readable on
mobile hardware.

Stage F – Optimization: - Use level streaming or room-based loading
where appropriate. - Use occlusion culling, LODs, texture atlases and
baked lighting where suitable. - Limit simultaneous dynamic lights and
expensive transparent fog effects. - Test on the minimum target Android
device.

Important: Do not generate the entire mansion as one uneditable static
mesh. Use modular, named, reusable room and corridor pieces. Keep doors,
puzzles, enemy triggers and quest objects as separate interactive game
objects.

The final result must be a navigable, playable interconnected mansion
level, not just a rendered picture or a collection of disconnected
rooms.
